## What we are proposing

A project to improve the security and reliability of professional communications, strengthen connections between colleagues and develop network expertise across ISRM. The application is an instrument for that organisational development, alongside agreed policies, responsibilities and incentives.

**Purpose:** Help ISRM turn professional enquiries into reasoned expert selection, agreed introductions and opportunities to collaborate — retaining sources, review findings and responsibility for recommendations.

**The graph helps make sense of it. OSINT helps prepare the information. Professional review provides a considered, accountable judgement. Subsequent interaction creates the result.**

The originating enquiry concerns research on approximately 11,000 people. A large delivery of dossiers does not itself establish demand, an appropriate contact route, willingness to participate or a client engagement. We can continue individual research while also showing how its output could be searched, reviewed, selected, maintained and used again. This is an extension of the practical question, not a claim that the original enquiry was wrong.

## Product hypothesis and pilot

We are testing whether the use of network expertise can become a managed process: source, date, digital checks, professional review, appropriate contact, willingness to participate and recorded outcome. The process should remain useful with or without AI assistance.

The difficult questions are maintaining current information and securing participation. Someone must own the process, apply the rules and have a reason to act. Technology can reduce friction but does not supply these conditions by itself. Shared standards need to define acceptable evidence and outcomes; willingness, recommendation and suitability remain distinct from an identity/source status.

ISRM is a plausible pilot setting because it already has chapters, professional activity and potential sponsors and reviewers. It is not automatically the best setting: the pilot needs a real enquiry, an accountable owner, willing participants and permitted data. Test the response of this actual community rather than assuming that association membership guarantees engagement.

Measure time to a useful shortlist, reviewer-confirmed relevance, record freshness, willingness to respond, completed next actions and repeat use. Record the current approach first. Do not promise conversion, revenue or an automatic increase in trust.

## Where demand is plausible

These are discovery hypotheses, not market-size estimates or evidence of willingness to pay.

| Potential buyer | Repeated useful job | What must be established |
|---|---|---|
| Professional associations and chapter networks | Respond to enquiries, locate relevant expertise and support appropriate introductions | A process owner, recurring demand, member benefit and capacity to maintain records |
| Specialist consulting and advisory networks | Build a suitable expert group, identify complementary expertise and record referrals | Commercial confidentiality, clear recommendation responsibility and a reason to contribute contacts |
| Research and cross-sector consortia | Find collaborators and assemble expertise for a defined enquiry or proposal | Field definitions, current availability, contribution rights and an existing process to improve |
| Humanitarian or resilience networks | Find relevant operational expertise across organisations | Permitted data, access boundaries, duty of care and evidence of a sustainable budget; sensitive context raises requirements rather than automatically increasing attractiveness |

The strongest initial segment is the one where a sponsor can name repeated enquiries, a costly coordination problem and people who will participate. A large directory without that operating demand is a weak sales target.

## How to promote a standalone application — three steps

1. **Demonstrate one familiar enquiry.** Find expertise, inspect its source and status, retain a shortlist and identify a permitted next action. Offer ordinary search and comparison as well as graph exploration; make the result understandable without graph expertise.
2. **Run a bounded paid pilot with a process owner.** Agree baseline, permitted sample, responsibilities, targets, operating effort and a decision date. Use measured outcomes and authorised testimonials/case material; distinguish a demonstration from a working shared deployment.
3. **Repeat the same job with a small set of comparable organisations.** Check which data model, workflow and controls repeat, and which require paid adaptation. Standardise the reusable core and connectors only after that evidence; consider a subscription model later, without assuming audience size proves a SaaS market.

## How our proposal compares with the market

The proposed differentiation is an accountable enquiry-to-interaction process, shaped around ISRM's network, rather than a claim to have invented graphs, OSINT, membership software or relationship intelligence. Value would come from connecting evidence, professional judgement, permission, willingness and follow-up within the association's working practices.

Sensitivity, ownership requirements and a core trusted-consultancy process are reasons to examine the implementation carefully. They do not alone prove bespoke software is safer or better value than an established service. Bespoke work is justified where required policies, evidence handling, user interaction or integrations cannot be met adequately through configuration and adaptation at an acceptable lifecycle cost.

Preserve standard systems where they do their job. Quote only the custom gap and the integration work needed to complete the agreed process. Compare build, adapt and buy against the same acceptance checks and three-year costs.

It is not directly comparable with a US-dollar subscription or an enterprise quotation that has not been obtained. The internal full-cost funding calculation and public planning figures must be reconciled before a fixed offer. ## Already created; not yet demonstrated as operational

The supplied corpus contains 178 records; 45 have the source flag `is_verified`. This is not proof that all are ISRM members, that every expertise claim is independently confirmed or that disclosure is authorised.

The current artefact has a graph, search, dossiers, browser-local shortlists/notes/export and a capability configurator that carries selections into a proposal request. It also contains a labelled walkthrough of a research process. Shared authenticated operations, general client-file import, business-card OCR, live external OSINT and a protected central audit trail must not be represented as a completed operational bundle.

The configurator is a real tool for shaping proposed scope, not the operational application and not an instruction to activate services. Its breadth allows ISRM to select support for its organisational development. Choices and tailored descriptions provide input to the architect and the detailed proposal; they do not prove demand or constitute acceptance of a final price.

# Counterparty audit: assessment against the current application

Prepared for internal discussion. Audience of the client material: Dr David Rubens D.SyRM, CSyP, F.ISRM, Executive Director of the ISRM, for the Strategic Board.

The supplied assessments are useful evidence of first-use misunderstanding, not a verified inventory of implementation or an independent market valuation. They describe /scope and /proposal inconsistently: the current /proposal is the six-slide briefing; /scope holds selected capabilities, indicative prices and proposal preparation. The configurator has its own labelled graph workspace.

| Supplied observation | Current evidence | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Search has no drill-down, shortlist, retention or export | Mini/full dossiers, selected-only groups, local snapshots and Markdown exist | Improve visibility and continuity; replace the obstructive horizontal tray with bounded vertical panels |
| The configurator is entirely disabled | Workflow selection, custom requests and the estimate work; bundled core capabilities and replay choices are intentionally not individually editable | Retain the bundle and replay boundaries; show clear intent and actual requirement-form validation |
| Board decision, ownership and acceptance are insufficiently evident | A focused pilot and three delivery stages already exist, but much responsibility context was in notes | Make the immediate problem, role-specific value and bounded pilot decision explicit in the six slides; add responsibility and acceptance context to scope and exports |
| A visually convincing prototype may set production expectations | Shared authenticated storage, connected research and operational integrations are not commissioned through the demonstration | Preserve clear prototype and proposed-capability labels; no new live-service claims |
| A shortlist should remain available during search | The horizontal tray reduced the usable graph area | Adjacent resizable list and dossier; compact screens switch panels and retain graph/search state |

## Suggestions adopted

- Retain the six-slide sequence rather than add three case slides.
- Frame the first decision as a bounded pilot with an ISRM sponsor, a reviewer, a real enquiry and a decision date.
- Compare time to a useful shortlist against current practice, relevance and willingness to respond. Set baselines and targets by agreement, not invented ROI.
- Specify proposed functional acceptance checks: inspect evidence/status, collect a selected group, resume search, retain/export permitted records, and test agreed access/correction/review arrangements before operational release.
- Keep the prototype for member use distinct from selecting proposed capabilities.

## Claims not adopted

- “Maximum trust”, blanket “verified community”, or the association's mandate as a guarantee of expertise or accuracy.
- Zero-Knowledge, GDPR compliance, data sovereignty or ownership assurances unsupported by an agreed architecture, operating model and legal arrangements.
- Automatic adverse decisions, sanctions-based blocking, or World Monitor/OpenSanctions already operating as individual verification. These require separately specified sources, matching, review, correction and decision responsibilities.
- “Two minutes”, guaranteed returns, or 5–10% technical completion and 70–80% perceived value without a baseline or measurement method.
- Assuming all frontend code must be rewritten. Reuse requires a developer review of components, data contracts, permissions and tests. Commit counts and lines of code alone cannot establish reusable percentage or effort.
- Unsubstantiated comparisons that LinkedIn, CRM data or particular expert networks necessarily have lower trust.
- New Essential/Professional/Enterprise packages before the core and optional workflows have a stable agreed scope and commercial position.

## Validation limits

Code paths and regression tests can confirm implemented selection and output logic. They do not establish first-use usability, production readiness or demand. Browser automation is unavailable in this session; live drag, focus and mobile visual checks remain to be confirmed. A short observed task with first-time users should assess whether they can distinguish the four stages and complete a search, retain a group and prepare a scope request without narration.
